Why the dye-lot is part of the key

Yarn is not a fungible commodity. Two lots of one nominal shade are dyed in separate baths, and the difference between them shows up as a band across the finished fabric at the point the warp or the feed changes over. Pooling them into a single “30s Navy” balance hides exactly the information a weaver needs, so this ledger never does it.

A lot is identified by count, shade, dye-lot and supplier together. Two suppliers reuse lot numbers, so the supplier has to be part of the identity. Capitalisation and stray spacing are ignored, so dl-1142 and DL-1142 stay on one balance.

Adjust a lot

Oldest first is not just an accounting convention. The lot dyed earliest has had the longest to drift in storage, so it is the one to clear first, and running one lot right down before opening the next keeps the number of shade joins in the cloth to a minimum.

Yarn count conversion

Ne and Nm are indirect counts: a bigger number is a finer yarn. Tex and denier are direct: a bigger number is a coarser yarn. Converting between the two families is a reciprocal, not a multiplication, which is the error this panel exists to prevent.

The conversion factors used

Everything above comes from three constants, with tex as the hub:

tex    = 1000 / Nm        Nm  = 1000 / tex
Ne     = 590.5 / tex      tex = 590.5 / Ne
denier = 9 x tex          tex = denier / 9

1000 and 9 are exact by definition. The Ne factor works out to 590.5412… from the definition of the hank, and mill practice rounds it to 590.5, which is the figure used here: conversions stay within 0.007% of exact. If your buyer works to a different rounding, convert with their factor before quoting.

A folded count divides in Ne and multiplies in tex: a two-fold of 60s singles is a resultant Ne 30 and a resultant 19.683 tex. Only the plies-first form is read. 60/2 means the same yarn in some trades, and guessing between the two notations would silently take a count from 30 to 0.033, so it is refused instead.

Import receipts from a CSV

Columns are matched by header name, not position, so your own sheet's column order is fine. date, count, shade, dye_lot and kg are required; supplier and ref are optional. Common alternatives are understood: colour, lot no, vendor, weight, invoice. Rows that cannot be read are listed with their line numbers and the rest still import.

Two percentages, because the floor uses both and they are not the same number. Against standard divides the excess by the standard requirement, which is the figure to judge a costing allowance by. Wastage on issue divides it by what was actually drawn, which is the conventional mill wastage figure. Either is left blank when its denominator is zero rather than shown as a nonsense number.
